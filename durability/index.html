Load & Durability

Who is likely to miss games to injury next season, where on the body it tends to happen, and which of the usual suspects (heavy minutes, back-to-backs, travel, miles run, coaches, college workload, body size) actually predict it. Every input had a pass/fail bar written down before it was tested.

What the official injury reports changed

Version 1 had to guess: any run of three or more missed games counted as an injury. The league's own injury reports, filed before every game from 2021-22 on, say why a player was out. Matched against those reports, the guess was mostly wrong.

Coaches: who rides his starters hardest

Coach load is how many minutes a night his five most-used players averaged, compared with the league that season, over every season he was a head coach since 2010-11. Rest is one- and two-game breaks per rotation player per season. Coach load did not forecast injuries at the season level (test L3), so this describes style.

Teams

Nobody publishes how teams train or rehab players, so these lines measure what the work produces. Injury games are from the official reports for 2024-25. A team's medical record did not repeat from one season to the next (test TM1), so read these as last season's luck as much as anything.

What this does not do

  • Season totals are still hard to forecast. A fuller season model (history, age, position, workload, contact, size, a tree model) did not beat last season's injury games alone. The number on the table is honest about that: it mostly says who was hurt recently.
  • Load works at the game level, but it is partly exposure. More minutes means more time on the floor to get hurt. The data cannot separate tired legs from simply playing more.
  • 2025-26 is partial. NBA.com's firewall blocks its 2026 report files, so the season is covered through December 21.
  • High school is not in it. There is no public record of high-school injuries or minutes. College is shown as workload and games missed, and it did not forecast.
  • Teams list what they choose to. "Injury management" on a back-to-back is often planned rest, and some injuries are listed vaguely or late.
  • The playoff tests use any games missed before 2021-22. Official injury reports only go back that far, so the 15-season playoff sample counts runs of three or more missed games for regulars, which were about 75% injuries when checked against the reports. A holdout or a suspension still counts.
  • It is a forecast for groups, not a diagnosis. A 50% chance of missing 20 games means half of players like him did.
