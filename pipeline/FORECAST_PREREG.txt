GHOST SCREEN SEASON FORECAST: rules written before any backtest was run (2026-10-14)

What it does
- Rates every player (points per 100 possessions above average, offense and defense separately).
- Turns ratings + projected minutes + expected availability into a team strength.
- Simulates the 2026-27 season 1,000,000 times every day: every remaining game, the play-in, the playoffs, the awards.

Player rating (fixed before testing)
- Regularized adjusted plus-minus from every lineup stint since 2010-11, offense and defense separately, three-season window
  with the older seasons down-weighted, shrunk toward a box-score prior (points, shooting, free throws, assists, turnovers,
  rebounds, steals, blocks per 100 possessions) instead of toward zero.
- The box-score prior's weights are fit on earlier seasons only when testing a later season (no peeking).
- Next-season rating = this rating plus an aging adjustment fit on earlier seasons.
- Rookies: rating from draft slot, fit on earlier rookie classes.

Tests (season held out = predicted using only seasons before it; seasons 2014-15 to 2025-26)
F1 Team strength. Predict each team's point differential per game for the coming season from the player projections and
   minutes from the previous season's rotation (minutes known before the season). Pass if the mean absolute error beats
   the simple baseline "last season's point differential x 0.6" by at least 5%.
F2 Game picks. Pick the winner of every regular-season game with the preseason team strengths + home court.
   Pass if the share picked correctly beats the same baseline's share and the log loss is better.
F3 Calibration. Group predicted game win probabilities into tenths; in every group with 300+ games, actual win rate within
   5 points of predicted. Fail if any group misses by more than 5 points.
F4 In-season blend. Strength after N games = preseason x K/(K+N) + results x N/(K+N). K chosen on 2014-15 to 2019-20
   only, then tested on 2020-21 to 2025-26: pass if blended beats both "preseason only" and "results only" in log loss
   on games after the 20th.
Awards (A1) Voter models fit on 2010-11 to 2019-20 award voting; tested on 2020-21 to 2025-26. Pass if the actual
   winner had the highest model probability in at least 4 of 6 seasons for MVP. Reported for each award, pass or fail.

Rules for publishing
- Anything that fails is still published with the forecast, labeled, and the forecast says so.
- 1,000,000 simulated seasons per day. Odds are shown to the nearest 0.1%; "<0.1%" and ">99.9%" at the edges.

RESULTS (2026-10-14, first run; nothing below changed the bars above)
F1 PASS  mean error 2.68 vs baseline 3.14 points per game (14.7% better), 2015-16 to 2025-26.
F2 PASS  63.4% of games picked right vs 61.3%; log loss 0.6375 vs 0.6531.
F3 PASS  largest calibration gap 2.7 points.
F4 PASS  K=20 (chosen on 2014-15..2019-20); 2020-21..2025-26 log loss blend 0.6212, preseason only 0.6516, results only 0.6239.
A1 FAIL  MVP winner had the top probability in 3 of 6 seasons (bar 4). Misses: 2020-21, 2021-22, 2023-24 (model's pick finished 2nd or 3rd).
   Reported: DPOY 3/6, ROY 3/6, Sixth Man 2/6, Most Improved 2/6, Coach of the Year 3/6.

Data fix (before the A1 numbers above): 2025-26 player names were missing from the converted play-by-play, so the
2025-26 voting could not be matched to players; names now come from every season's names, the rosters and the injury page.
First A1 run with the bug: MVP 2 of 6.

Choices made after the tests, for the award odds only (no test result depends on them):
- "Rookie" means a player who has never played an NBA game (a returning veteran from overseas is not a rookie).
- A player with no starts history: top-10 picks are assumed to start 60% of games, everyone else 25%.
- Scoring in each simulated season varies more for younger players (1.2 points + 0.35 per year under 27) and moves with
  the projected change in offensive rating; without this the Most Improved odds went to stars by construction.
- Award odds use 100,000 of the 1,000,000 seasons.
Simplifications: ties in the standings are broken by point differential; the two NBA Cup knockout/makeup games each team
has without a fixed opponent are played against a league-average team on a neutral floor.

ADDITIONS (written 2026-10-14, before any of these tests were run)
Training seasons for every fit below: 2014-15 to 2019-20. Test seasons: 2020-21 to 2025-26. Game-level tests are
scored on log loss of the home team winning, starting from the preseason strengths (F2) plus home court.

S1 Schedule factor. Each game gets an adjustment from: rest days for each team (0 = second night of a back-to-back,
   1, 2, 3+), the third game in four nights, miles traveled since the last game, time zones crossed, and playing at
   altitude (Denver, Utah). Pass if test-season log loss improves by at least 0.2% (relative).
S2 Projected health. Team strength with each player's minutes weighted by his projected share of games played
   (last three seasons, shrunk to the league norm) vs. not weighted. Scored on F1 (team point differential error).
   Pass if the error drops by at least 2% on test seasons.
S3 Surprise factor. How far a team can land from its projection: the spread of (actual - projected) point
   differential is modeled from roster continuity (share of last season's minutes returning), average age weighted by
   minutes, and minutes going to players with no NBA history. Fit on training seasons; pass if, on test seasons,
   the per-team spread gives a better log score for actual differentials than one spread for every team (any gain).
   Also reported: whether young or high-turnover teams beat their projections on average (a mean shift), used only
   if it improves the same score.
S4 Late season. In each team's last 15 games, teams already out of the race (bottom 6 in the league by record at
   that point) and teams with their seed nearly locked (top 2 in their conference with a 5+ game cushion): do they
   play worse than their current strength says? Pass (and used in the simulation) if the shift is at least
   1.5 points per game with a 95% range that excludes 0, measured on all seasons 2014-15 to 2025-26.
Tried after S3 (and dropped): giving players with no NBA history only the minutes the veterans leave, because deep
teams (OKC) were credited with many rookie minutes. It made F1 worse (error 2.73 vs 2.68) and F2 worse (62.7% vs 63.4%),
so the original minutes stay.

RESULTS FOR S1-S4 (2026-10-14, first run)
S1 PASS  log loss 0.6436 vs 0.6465 (0.46% better; bar 0.2%), 7171 test games.
   Fitted on all seasons (points for the home team): second night of a back-to-back -1.88 (difference between teams),
   3rd game in 4 nights -0.66, each time zone crossed -0.27, altitude (Denver/Utah home) +1.87;
   home court with these in the model 1.86.
S2 FAIL  weighting minutes by projected games played: error 3.035 vs 3.028 (-0.2%).
   So projected health does not move expected strength; each simulated season still draws who is healthy (injury luck),
   relative to each player's own projection. Games actually missed this season, and players out right now, do count.
S3 PASS  log score 510.8 (per-team spread) and 510.1 (spread + shift) vs 514.8
   (one spread for all; lower is better). Shift used: per standard deviation, continuity +0.29, age
   +0.07, rookie minutes -0.52 points per game. Spread grows with rookie minutes.
S4 PASS  out of the race: -3.02 points per game in the last 15 (95% -3.99 to -2.06,
   81 team-seasons); seed locked: -2.45 (-4.10 to -0.80, 29).
   In the simulation the standings that decide this are taken when each team's last 15 games begin.
