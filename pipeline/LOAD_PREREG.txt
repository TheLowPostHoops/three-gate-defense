LOAD & DURABILITY MODEL: tests written before any model was fit (2026-10-12)
==========================================================================

Question: can a player's workload, mileage, age and absence history forecast how many games he misses next season,
and does the way his team (its coach) runs its starters add anything?

DATA
- NBA play-by-play (shufinskiy/nba_data), 2010-11 through 2025-26, regular season and playoffs. Rebuilt lineups give
  each player's seconds and possessions on the floor in every game. Game dates from shot detail.
- Age, position and career minutes before 2010-11 from a public Basketball-Reference season table
  (gabriel1200/site_Data, tplayer_stats.csv, through 2023-24; later ages carried forward by one year per season).
- Head coaches: pipeline/coaches.json (written by hand, checked against Wikipedia's current list).
- NOT available: high-school and college injuries, injury type or body part, official injury reports, tracking
  distance (miles run). The model cannot use them and the page must say so.

OUTCOME (season t+1)
- Missed games = regular-season games his team played while he was with it and he did not play, counted only in runs
  of 3 or more straight games (shorter runs are mostly rest). "With it" = from his team's first game (first team of the
  season) or his first game after a trade, through the season's end or his last game before a trade.
- Scaled to an 82-game season. Players who never play in season t+1 are left out (cannot separate a season-long injury
  from retirement, waivers or playing abroad); the count is reported.
- This is games missed for any reason in runs of 3+: mostly injury and illness, but also suspensions, personal leave,
  health-and-safety protocols (2020-22) and end-of-season shutdowns. Season averages are absorbed by a season term.
- Binary version: missed 20+ games (scaled).

SAMPLE
- Player-season pairs with 1,000+ regular-season minutes in season t, t = 2012-13 ... 2024-25 (13 pairs; three prior
  seasons of play-by-play history needed). Out-of-sample by leaving one target season out at a time.

FEATURE BLOCKS (all known before season t+1 starts)
- H history: missed-game share in t, t-1, t-2; number of 10+ game runs missed over t-2..t.
- A age next season (and age squared).
- M mileage: career regular-season minutes through t; playoff minutes in t.
- W workload in t: minutes per game played, possessions per minute on the floor (pace of his minutes: the closest
  thing to "running" in this data), share of games with 38+ minutes, back-to-back second nights with 30+ minutes
  per game played, biggest 7-day minutes spike vs his 28-day average.
- P position group (guard / wing / big from listed position).
- T team load: minutes per game of the top-5 minutes players on his next team, measured in season t (the coach's
  habit).
  Amendment 2026-10-12, before any fit: the "coach changed (yes/no)" input is dropped, because a full historical coach
  table for all 30 teams is not available; coaches.json covers only the 31 coaches named on the page.

TESTS (bars fixed now)
L1 Forecast: full model (H+A+M+W+P+T), leave-one-season-out.
   (a) typical miss (mean absolute error, games per 82) beats "last season's missed games, pulled 50% toward average"
       by at least 0.50 games.
   (b) Brier score for missing 20+ games beats the same baseline turned into a probability (logistic on that single
       input) by at least 2% (relative).
L2 Workload and mileage: adding W+M to H+A+P improves the 20+ game Brier score by at least 1% (relative) AND the
   typical miss by at least 0.20 games. If it fails, workload is shown as a description, not used in the risk number.
L3 Coach / team load: adding T to everything else improves the Brier score by at least 0.5% (relative). If it fails,
   coach load is shown as a description only and the page says it did not forecast injuries.
L4 Range check: 80% ranges for games missed contain the actual result for 74-86% of player-seasons.
L5 Calibration: in each fifth of predicted 20+ game risk, predicted and actual rates within 5 points.

DESCRIPTIVE (no pass/fail): league pace and minute concentration by season; coach load table.

Shipping rule: only blocks that pass their test enter the published risk number. Every result is published, pass or fail.

AMENDMENT 2026-10-12 (after the first run; reported with both versions)
- Found in a sanity check after the first run: a player hurt when traded had his absence dropped, because his new
  team's window started at his first game there (Doncic 2024-25 counted 5 missed games, Durant 2022-23 10). Fix: the
  new team's games between his last game for the old team and his first game for the new one count as missed, minus
  2 games for the trade itself, when that leaves a run of 3+. First-run results stay in the record:
  L1 MAE 12.46 baseline vs 12.94 full (FAIL), Brier 0.1981 vs 0.1956 (1.3%, FAIL); L2 FAIL; L3 FAIL.

L6 (written before the miles data was joined): NBA.com tracking miles, 2013-14 on. Inputs: miles per 36 minutes on
   the floor, average speed, miles per game, biggest 7-day miles spike vs 28-day average (from game-by-game miles).
   Sample: pairs with tracking in season t (t = 2013-14 ... 2024-25). Adding the miles block to the full model improves
   the 20+ game Brier score by at least 1% (relative). Otherwise miles are shown as a description only.

TEAM TESTS (written 2026-10-12 before any team-level number was computed). No public data describes how teams train
or rehab players, so these measure what the staff's work produces.
TM1 Team health effect: per team-season, average (actual - model-expected) missed games of its 1,000+ minute players
    (out-of-sample expectations). Repeats season to season (same team, consecutive seasons) at r >= 0.20 -> shown as
    a team line; also tested as a model input: adding last season's team effect for his next team improves the 20+
    game Brier score by >= 0.5% (relative). Otherwise description only.
TM2 Rehab: re-absence = a new run of 3+ missed games starting within 15 games of returning from a 10+ game run.
    Team re-absence rate repeats at r >= 0.20 -> shown as a team line. Player-level: a re-absence in the last two
    seasons as a model input, Brier gain >= 0.5% (relative).
TM3 Rest policy: 1-2 game absences of rotation players per player-season, by team. Descriptive only.
C1 College (Bart Torvik player files, 2008 on, matched by name): college minutes, share of team games missed in
    college, years in college, height. Sample: NBA pairs with a matched college record (and a no-college flag for the
    rest). Adding the college block improves the 20+ game Brier score by >= 1% (relative). Otherwise description only.
    Ages from Torvik birth dates replace estimated ages where available (data fix, not a test).

DATA FIXES 2026-10-12 (after the tests above were first run; results reported both ways on the page)
- NBA.com 2025-26 speed/distance has 44 player rows with impossible average speeds (13-22 mph). Rows over 5.5 mph are
  dropped (11 more across 2013-25). First L6 run with them: Brier 0.2033 -> 0.2042 (worse, FAIL).
- Position groups: a listed PF is a wing, only C / PF-C is a big (Butler and George were grouped as bigs).
- Projections are published only for players with 1,000+ minutes last season, the same floor as the sample
  (500 let in two-way players whose G League stints counted as missed games).
- A season with no games between two seasons a player did play now counts as 82 missed games in his history
  (Leonard 2021-22, Thompson 2019-21 had been treated as unknown). Results before this fix: L1 Brier 0.2043 -> 0.2011
  (1.6%, FAIL), shipped model AUC 0.603.

VERSION 2 (written 2026-10-12, before any v2 number was computed)
==========================================================================
New data: the NBA's official injury reports (ak-static.cms.nba.com, one per game day, 2021-22 through 2025-26).
Each listed player has a status (Out, Doubtful, Questionable, Probable, Available) and a reason: Injury/Illness
with body part and type, Rest, G League, Personal, Suspension, Health and Safety Protocols, Not with team, etc.

Injury games missed = games he did not play while listed Out or Doubtful with an Injury/Illness reason.
Body part = the part named in that reason; groups: ankle/foot, knee, hamstring/calf/thigh/groin/hip (lower-body
muscle), back, shoulder/arm/hand/wrist/finger, head/concussion, illness, other.

V0 (check of version 1, no bar): share of version 1's "missed games" (runs of 3+) that were injury games; share of
injury games version 1 missed (runs of 1-2). Reported as is.

V1 Season forecast on the true outcome: next-season injury games missed (scaled to 82), pairs t = 2021-22..2024-25,
   1,000+ minutes. History (injury games in t, t-1, t-2; version-1 missed games before 2021-22), age, position.
   Beats last season's injury games alone by >= 2% (relative) on the 20+ injury game Brier score. Leave one target
   season out.
V2 Game-level load: unit = a player-game he started healthy (played his team's previous game). Event = an injury
   absence starts at his next team game (he misses it while listed Out/Doubtful with Injury/Illness, excluding
   illness). Base inputs: his injury events per game over the prior two seasons, age, position, games since his last
   injury return. Load inputs: minutes in the last 7 and 28 days and their ratio, minutes in his last game, second
   night of a back-to-back, games in the last 7 days, season minutes so far. Pass if the load inputs improve
   out-of-sample log loss by >= 0.5% (relative), leaving one season out. The relative risk for the top tenth of the
   7-day/28-day ratio is reported either way.
V3 Same body part: after a return from a 3+ game injury absence, the chance that his next injury within 30 team games
   is the same body part group. Pass if the same-group rate is >= 2x what the league mix of body parts would give,
   with a 95% interval above 1. Separately: adding "lower-body muscle injuries in the last two seasons" to V1
   improves the Brier score by >= 1% (relative).
V4 Model form: gradient-boosted trees vs. the logistic/Poisson model on V1's inputs. Switch only if Brier improves
   >= 1% (relative).
Teams: 2026-27 rosters replace 2025-26 teams where available (data fix, not a test).

V2 addendum (written 2026-10-12 before these inputs were joined to any outcome)
- Report coverage: NBA.com blocked its 2026 report files (Akamai "Access Denied"), so official labels run from
  2021-22 through mid-December 2025. The 2025-26 outcome is scaled by the games covered.
V5 Travel (game level, added to V2): miles flown and time zones crossed in the last 7 days, altitude game, days of
   rest before the next game. Pass if log loss improves >= 0.5% (relative).
V6 Contact exposure: prior-season shots at the rim, free throws, fouls drawn and charges taken, each per 36 minutes.
   Pass if V1 Brier improves >= 1% or V2 log loss improves >= 0.5% (relative); report both.
V7 Body size: height, weight, BMI (NBA.com bios; listed weight is the latest one, not the weight in that season).
   Same bars as V6.
Game-by-game miles were not completed (NBA.com stats throttling), so no game-level miles test is run.
- Fixes after the first v2 run (2026-10-12; first-run numbers kept in the record): (a) the game-level load windows
  had included minutes from the game the injury happened in (a player hurt mid-game plays fewer minutes), which leaks
  the answer; windows now end before that game and "minutes in his last game" means the game before. First run:
  log loss 0.13696 -> 0.13245 (3.3%), travel 0.8%. (b) "Injury management" listings (planned rest of a known injury,
  mostly on back-to-backs) are kept in the event as pre-registered, and a stricter version without them is reported.
  (c) Report names matched by last name within the team-season when the full name failed.

PLAYOFF LOAD (written 2026-10-13, before any playoff-vs-next-season number was computed)
==========================================================================
Question (from the article draft): do playoff games, or several years of long runs, lead to more games missed the
next season?

Check done before writing this (not a playoff number): among 2021-22..2025-26 player-seasons, the share of version-1
missed games (runs of 3+) that were official injury games is 9% for players under 15 min/game, 42% at 15-25, 69% at
25-30 and 76% at 30+. So the long-history tests use rotation regulars only, where the version-1 count is mostly injury.

SAMPLE
- Long history (H): seasons t = 2010-11 .. 2024-25, players with 1,500+ regular-season minutes and 25+ min/game in
  season t who play in season t+1. Outcome: version-1 missed games in t+1 (runs of 3+, scaled to 82).
- Official labels (R): t = 2021-22 .. 2024-25, players with 1,000+ minutes; outcome: official injury games in t+1.
- Binary outcome: 20+ games missed next season.

EXPOSURES (season t unless noted)
- Team playoff games: how many playoff games his team played while he was on it (0 = missed the playoffs). This is
  the main exposure because it does not depend on whether he got hurt during the playoffs (a player hurt in the
  playoffs logs few playoff minutes, which would hide any effect).
- His playoff minutes (secondary).
- Three-year load: team playoff games summed over t-2, t-1, t.
- Short offseason: days from his team's last game to the next season's opener (descriptive only).

CONTROLS: regular-season minutes and min/game in t, age next season, position, his missed games in t, t-1.

TESTS
P1 Thresholds (the binary version): rate of 20+ missed games next season by team playoff games in t: none, 1-6
   (first-round exit), 7-13 (second round), 14+ (conference finals or further). Raw rates with 95% intervals, and
   the same after adjusting for the controls (logistic regression). Pass if the adjusted odds ratio for 14+ vs none
   is above 1 with its 95% interval above 1.
P2 Does it forecast? Adding team playoff games (t) to the controls improves the out-of-sample (leave one season out)
   Brier score for 20+ games by >= 0.5% (relative). Same bar, separately, for the three-year load.
P3 Same player, different years: a player-fixed-effects regression (each player compared with himself) of next-season
   missed games on team playoff games in t, with the controls. Pass if the coefficient is positive with a 95%
   interval (clustered by player) above 0.
P4 Official labels: P1's adjusted odds ratio and P2 on sample R. Reported; R is small (4 outcome seasons), so it is a
   check on H, not its own pass/fail.
Descriptive: playoff vs regular-season minutes per game for each team's top five; the Warriors' core 2014-15 ..
2018-19 (playoff games by season and games missed the next season).
- Data fix 2026-10-13 (before the playoff tests were run): ages now come from NBA.com birth dates for every player
  who has one (age on Feb 1 of the season). The earlier estimate (Basketball-Reference season age carried forward,
  Torvik birth dates by name) was off by a year for many players and badly wrong for a few name collisions
  (Jalen Williams shown 29, is 25; Brandon Miller shown 27, is 24). Version 2 tests are rerun with the fixed ages.

DATA FIX 2026-10-13 (ages): 93 of 332 published ages were wrong. Some came from college birthdates matched by name
that belonged to a different player (Jalen Williams shown as 29, Brandon Miller as 27); the rest were one year off
from carrying Basketball-Reference ages forward. Ages now come from NBA.com birthdates by player id (age on Feb 1 of
the season), and a college record is dropped when its birthdate disagrees with NBA.com's. All tests that use age
were rerun; results before the fix stay in this file and in the page's history.
DATA FIX 2026-10-13 (games played): checked against NBA.com's official 2025-26 totals for all 582 players. Players
with 1,000+ minutes: minutes within 0.03% on average, games played exact for 90% (the rest off by 1-3). Below 500
minutes games played were often short, because the 2025-26 source (converted cdn play-by-play) has no team id for the
player coming in on a substitution, so a player who checked in and recorded nothing was missed. Fixed by giving him
the team of the player going out.
P5 (added 2026-10-13 after P1-P4 were run, so it is a check, not a pre-set test): the P1-P4 sample leaves out players
who played no games in season t+1, which drops the worst outcomes (Thompson 2019-20, Durant 2019-20). P5 reruns P1
and P3 counting a season with no games as 82 missed when the player played again in a later season.
