How the odds have moved

Player ratings

Points per 100 possessions a player adds to his team's margin, compared with an average player, split into offense and defense. It comes from every lineup since 2010-11 and is anchored by box score stats. These ratings drive the team strengths above.

How it works

Players. Each player's rating comes from every lineup he has played in over the last three seasons, the most recent weighted most. It is anchored to his box score stats, then adjusted for age. A rookie starts from what players taken in his draft slot have done in their first year.

Teams. A team's strength is its players' ratings weighted by expected minutes. We tested whether lowering a team's strength for injury-prone players helped. It didn't (test S2), so it isn't used. Injury luck is simulated instead: in each season some players stay healthy and some don't, around each player's own track record.

Schedule. Every game is adjusted for rest (the second night of a back-to-back costs about 1.9 points), a third game in four nights, time zones crossed, and the altitude in Denver and Utah (test S1).

Surprises. Each simulated season gives every team a luck draw. The size of that draw depends on how many minutes are returning, roster age and rookie minutes (test S3).

The last 15 games. Teams already out of the race played about 3 points worse than their strength over their last 15 games, and teams with their seed locked about 2.4 points worse (test S4). The simulation applies both based on where each team stands when its last 15 begin.

Seasons. In each of the 1,000,000 simulations, every player gets an availability draw (some seasons he stays healthy, some he doesn't) and every team gets a luck draw. Then every remaining game is played: home court is worth about 2.2 points, and a typical game swings about 13 points either way. Ties in the standings are broken by point differential, a simplification of the league's tiebreakers. The play-in and every playoff round are played out game by game, home court included. The lottery uses the league's odds for the #1 pick.

During the season. Results move a team's strength. After 20 games a team is judged half on its preseason rating and half on how it has actually played (adjusted for opponents and home court). After 60 games, mostly on results. Box scores update minutes, team changes, games played and players listed out injured.

What it can't see

  • Trades and signings after preseason show up once a player appears in a box score for his new team.
  • Injury news. The model knows who sat with an injury in his team's last game, but not how long the team expects him to be out.
  • Motivation. Late-season tanking and resting are modeled as an average effect (S4), not team by team. Contract years are not modeled.
  • Award voters also weigh storylines. The voter model picked the actual MVP in 3 of 6 recent seasons (it needed 4 to pass its test), and the misses were close runners-up. Treat award odds as rough.
  • These are Ghost Screen Analytics estimates built from public data, not NBA numbers.
